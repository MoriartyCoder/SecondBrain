\section{Super Resolution}

\label{sec:sr}

  

\subsection{Motivation and Task Definition}

  

Acquiring functional MRI (fMRI) at a lower field strength or with shorter

scans saves energy and widens access, but it lowers spatial resolution.

Super-resolution (SR) recovers a high-resolution (HR) volume from a

low-resolution (LR) acquisition, so the resolution penalty of the cheaper

acquisition can be paid back computationally rather than on the scanner.

  

SR is one stage of a larger restoration pipeline; it operates along the

spatial axis, taking a single degraded volume and predicting its HR

counterpart on a fixed grid:

\[

V_{\mathrm{LR}}\;(64{\times}64{\times}46) \;\rightarrow\; \hat{V}_{\mathrm{HR}}\;(128{\times}128{\times}93),

\]

using the acquired HR volume $V_{\mathrm{HR}}$ as the target. LR inputs are

generated by the k-space degradation of Section~\ref{sec:degradation}

($1.5\,\mathrm{mm}\rightarrow3.0\,\mathrm{mm}$, a $2\times$ factor), so

supervised pairs come directly from the data with no manual annotation. The

reference baseline is trilinear interpolation to the HR grid, a strong,

learning-free anchor that every model must beat.

  

Single-image SR architectures cluster into a few families. Project

constraints---a modest volumetric dataset and a limited VRAM/compute

budget---rule out the data-hungry, memory-heavy Transformer family

(e.g.\ SwinIR) and the unstable, potentially hallucinating GAN family

(e.g.\ SRGAN); for medical imaging the latter is disqualifying, since invented

texture is a clinical risk. We therefore follow the two CNN families that are

stable to train and faithful to the input: the plain convolutional lineage of

SRCNN~\cite{dong2016srcnn} and the attention-augmented residual lineage of

RCAN~\cite{zhang2018rcan}.

  

\subsection{Model and Training}

  

All models share one framework: the LR volume is

first pre-up-sampled to the fixed HR grid ($128{\times}128{\times}93$, from the

data manifest) with trilinear interpolation, then a convolutional network

refines it. The residual variants add a global skip, so the network only

learns the correction on top of the interpolation. This makes trilinear

interpolation an explicit, learning-free baseline that every model must beat,

and lets us read the genuine network contribution as the gap to that baseline

rather than from the raw loss.

  

\paragraph{Baseline.} \texttt{SRCNN3D-Classic} is the original SRCNN

$9$--$1$--$5$ layout~\cite{dong2016srcnn} ported to 3D (trilinear upsample then

three convolutions, no global skip, $\sim$53k parameters). It is reported for

reference only and not discussed further.

  

\paragraph{SRCNN3D-Deep.} Our main lightweight model deepens the SRCNN feature

path: the wide $9^3$ extractor is replaced by three stacked $3^3$ convolutions

and the $1^3$ mapping by a $3^3$ one, with \texttt{LeakyReLU}$(0.1)$ and a

global residual skip ($\sim$282k parameters). The receptive field stays at 13

voxels, but the extra non-linearities and the spatial (vs.\ $1^3$) mapping

raise representational capacity at higher per-voxel cost. The reconstruction

head is initialised with std $0.001$, so at step~0 the output equals the

trilinear baseline and optimisation starts from a stable point.

  

\paragraph{RCAN3D.} Our highest-capacity model is a 3D port of

RCAN~\cite{zhang2018rcan}. A head convolution lifts the upsampled volume to

$n_{\mathrm{feats}}$ channels; the body is a stack of residual groups, each a

sequence of Residual Channel-Attention Blocks (RCAB: conv--ReLU--conv followed

by a squeeze-and-excitation channel gate) plus a group-level skip; a tail

convolution maps back to one channel. The channel attention lets the network

re-weight feature maps adaptively---useful for suppressing noise and

emphasising anatomy---and the nested (block, group, global) skips keep

gradients flowing through the depth. We use $n_{\mathrm{feats}}=48$, 2 groups,

3 blocks and reduction~8 ($\sim$940k parameters), the largest configuration

that fits the VRAM budget at batch size~3.

  

All learned models are trained under the same recipe: a subject-level 80/20

split, $48^3$ patches (32 per volume), the Adam(W) optimiser with a plateau

scheduler, learning rate $3{\times}10^{-4}$, for 60 epochs. Losses operate

where they matter: a masked image-domain term restricts the objective to the

$\sim$34\% brain voxels, optionally combined with a k-space (Fourier-domain)

term to preserve high-frequency detail. SRCNN3D-Deep and RCAN3D use this

dual-domain masked loss; PSNR and SSIM are reported as evaluation metrics only,

never optimised.

  

\subsection{Evaluation and Results}

  

All models were evaluated on the held-out validation split with brain-masked

PSNR, SSIM and MSE, so the metrics reflect tissue rather than background.

Figure~\ref{fig:sr_ladder} shows every learned model clears the trilinear

baseline by a wide margin and improves monotonically with capacity:

SRCNN3D-Deep adds $+1.2$\,dB masked PSNR over the residual SRCNN3D baseline for

$5\times$ the parameters, and RCAN3D adds a further $+1.7$\,dB, reaching

$27.1$\,dB / $0.877$ SSIM---a $+7.9$\,dB / $+0.27$ SSIM gain over trilinear.

Figure~\ref{fig:sr_qual} makes the effect tangible: from the blurred

low-resolution input, SRCNN3D-Deep restores gross structure while RCAN3D

recovers finer cortical detail, closely approaching the high-resolution ground

truth.

\subsection{Role in the restoration pipeline}

  

Placed in the full IBC restoration pipeline (Fig.~\ref{fig:sr_pipeline}), SR

alone (RCAN3D) yields the best end-to-end fidelity and temporal SNR (tSNR),

ahead of a joint model on noisy LR, a denoise$\rightarrow$SR cascade, and

trilinear. SR is thus most effective as a dedicated spatial-restoration stage

rather than folded into a noisier joint objective.

\subsection{Conclusion}

  

Under a tight compute budget, faithful CNN super-resolution is preferable to

GAN/Transformer alternatives for 3D fMRI. RCAN3D gives the best fidelity, while

SRCNN3D-Deep recovers most of that gain at a third of the parameters---the

better accuracy-per-cost choice when resources are scarce. Both clearly surpass

the trilinear baseline, learning genuine high-frequency structure rather than

re-smoothing the input.
